Symmetrical figures are beautiful—and they are the subject of this task. A region in a 2D plane is convex
if, for every pair of points p and q in the region, the segment connecting p and q is entirely included in
the region. Also, a region in a 2D plane is point-symmetric if, when you rotate the region by 180 degrees
around a certain point, the rotated region exactly matches the original region.
You are given a convex polygon in a 2D plane with n vertices, numbered from 1 to n in counterclockwise
order. Vertex i has coordinates (xi , yi ). No three vertices are collinear. Determine whether there exists
a convex, point-symmetric region containing all of the n vertices on its boundary. If one or more such
regions exist, compute the minimum area among all of them.

Input
The first line of input contains one integer n (3 ≤ n ≤ 30). Each of the next n lines contains two integers.
The i-th line contains xi and yi (0 ≤ xi , yi ≤ 1000).
It is guaranteed that the given polygon is convex, its vertices are given in counterclockwise order, and no
three of its vertices are collinear.

Output
If one or more such regions exist, output the minimum area among all of them. The relative error of the
output must be within 10−9 .
If such a region does not exist, output -1 instead.
