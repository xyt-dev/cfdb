**Div2 B.** You should check for every circle of one ring: it have intersections with another ring or not. So, there are 4 checks.

There are 2 cases:
1. circle is inside of ring;
2. circle is outside of ring and ring is outside of circle;
3. circle is outside of ring and ring is inside of circle.
If at least one of these cases is performed, circle is good.

You can easily do checks following way. Let us *d* be a distance between centers of ring and circle, *r*~1~ and *R*~1~ are inside and outside radii of ring, *r* be radius of circle. Then conditions for all cases will be
1. *d* + *r* ≤ *r*~1~.
2. *r* + *R*~1~ ≤ *d*.
3. *d* + *R*~1~ ≤ *r*.
You can check all conditions in integers using squares of distances.

**Div2 C.** **Div1 A.**
The first solution. Consider sequence *a*~0~ = 1, *a*~*i*~ = *a*~*i* - 1~*k* + *b*:
*a*~0~, *a*~1~, *a*~2~, ..., *a*~*n*~ = *z*.
You can see that for all numbers from segment [*a*~0~, *a*~1~ - 1] you can number not less than *z* using exactly *n* steps. But for *n* - 1 steps you will bet number less than *z*. It works because transformation is monotonous. Analogically, for numbers from segments [*a*~1~, *a*~2~ - 1], [*a*~2~, *a*~3~ - 1], etc, you need exactly *n* - 1, *n* - 2, etc steps. So you just need find segment that contains number *t*. You can do it by generate a few first members of the sequence *a*. You need no more than *t* members.

The second solution. Equation:
*tk*^*x*^ + *b*(*k*^*x* - 1^ + *k*^*x* - 2^... + 1) ≥ *k*^*n*^ + *b*(*k*^*x* - 1^ + *k*^*x* - 2^... + 1)
Using formula for geometric progression you can get:

For *k* ≠ 1 we can multiply both sides by *k* - 1, (you can consider case *k* = 1 by yourself).
*t*(*k* - 1)*k*^*x*^ + *bk*^*x*^ - *b* ≥ (*k* - 1)*k*^*n*^ + *bk*^*n*^ - *b*

*k*^*x*^(*t*(*k* - 1) + *b*) ≥ *k*^*n*^(*k* - 1 + *b*)
